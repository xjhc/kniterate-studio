# Cashmere triangle with a paisley-size A and C3 decreases

Use the 406-pass command.kc with the usual draw thread in C1, one end of 3/28 Camel in C3, one end of 3/28 Fig in C4, and waste in C6. Start on an empty bed with the feeders at home on the left. The cashmere body uses stitch size 6.

The A matches the reference grid of 8 needles × 10 courses, with two-stitch legs and a two-course crossbar. At the measured cashmere gauge the footprint is approximately 1.8 cm wide × 1.7 cm tall with the cast-on below. The body is 50 courses, about 8.3 cm long and 3.0 cm deep. It casts on at three columns, widens ten times to 13, and narrows ten times to three.

| Passes | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1–168 | Waste, sacrificial starter, draw and three-column cast-on |
| 169–271 | Three tip courses, then C4 joined increases to 11 columns |
| 272–285 | Increase to 12 columns. First two courses of the letter |
| 286–297 | Six extra straight courses for the crossbar and shoulders |
| 298–311 | Final increase and completion at 13 columns. The letter finishes |
| 312–319 | First C3 decrease. Check the two consecutive Camel edge stitches on the Fig face at the turn |
| 320–377 | Continue to three columns, with C3 on the front edge of each decrease |
| 378–406 | Top waste, release and exit |

After knitting, secure the six live tip loops before removing the top waste. Check the relaxed letter upright and compare the joined edge on both faces at the turn. The extra six courses at width 12 make a short straight section on the widening slope; compare the stitches immediately at the turn when judging the C3 color change.

The machine check and loop replay passed for this file. Replay verified the letter stitches and the transfers, splits, edge joins and feeder assignments. No float-distance advisories. The project has a separate four-stitch-letter C3 file for a direct edge-only comparison with the four-stitch C4 control.
